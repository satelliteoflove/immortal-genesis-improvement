2026-11-18 21:08

A trial build draws in-between pictures: about 24 a second at the same game speed

This is the build to judge by eye. tools/build_rom.py --smooth writes build/immortal-smooth.md beside the normal build, which is unchanged. The game still updates 12 times a second. Each update, the trial draws one extra picture with everything, camera included, part of the way from where it was to where it's going, then the real picture 2 frames later. In the clips the normal build is on the left and the trial on the right, on the same scripted input. On a level 4 walk with the camera following, the picture changed 136 times in 680 frames on the left and 262 times on the right. Fights can't be smoothed this way. A fight runs in its own loop, and the two fighters stand at fixed places and only change pose, so there's nothing to draw in between. The trial leaves fights alone. The fight clip is there to show they still run. What it costs: the real picture now waits for frame 4 of the update, where the normal build usually has it up by frame 1 or 2. So a button press shows 2 to 3 frames later in full, though most of the movement shows at frame 2. Late updates went from 1.5% to 1.9%. What hasn't been looked at: how it feels to play, whether the wizard seems to slide (he moves in smaller steps but his legs change no more often), spells, deaths, traps and message text, sampled sound on levels 3, 4 and 7, and real hardware. The clips need a 60 Hz screen to show the difference fairly. docs/smoother-movement.md has the rest.

2026-11-18 20:20

The optimizing pass is done: 91% of updates are ready in 2 frames

Two more changes went in, and then the list ran out. The sound link, which copies 48 bytes out of the Z80's memory and back on every frame, has its copies written out in place of loops. That saves about 900 cycles a frame. It also stops the Z80 for about half as long, so sampled sounds on levels 3, 4 and 7 should have shorter hitches and run about 0.7% faster. Nobody has listened to that yet, and it's the one change so far with a known effect on output that hasn't been judged. There's a new measure, and it's the one to go by when the update rate comes up: how many video frames an update takes to get its picture on screen. The game allows 5. In the original, three quarters of updates needed 3 or more. Now 44% are ready in 1 frame, 47% in 2 and 8.5% in 3. Nearly everything still taking 3 is an update where the camera moved a step. The sprite tile upload looked like the biggest cost left, but it runs while the game would be waiting for vertical blank anyway, so moving it wouldn't finish updates any sooner. What's left after that is the music player, the game logic (spread thinly over 281 routines) and a rework of scrolling, none of which is worth the risk for what it would return. docs/optimizations.md has the full list of what wasn't done and why. Seen on the way, and not followed up: the game often finishes drawing a little after vertical blank has ended, which may or may not show at the top of the screen.

2026-11-18 19:39

Sprite masking and camera steps cost about half, and a mask bug is fixed

Three more changes, all proven against the original routines on random inputs and again during play. Sprite masking, which hides the parts of sprites behind scenery, now reads its shifted masks from tables built from your ROM. It costs about half what it did. That work turned up a bug in the original. The right-hand half of each mask was shifted one bit short, so a pixel or so along a mask's edge came out in the wrong colour, such as a yellow dot at the wizard's foot where he stands behind a floor edge. It showed in about 13% of frames in the walks. It's fixed, and the picture shows before and after. The fix is one number in tools/build_rom.py if it should ever need to come back out. Camera steps, the heaviest updates left, went from about 223k cycles to 133k. The routine that works out what's on screen is rewritten, and the tile table is scanned only where it has something in it. Late updates are 1.7%, nearly all deliberate waits in animations. The better measure now is how much of each update's time its work needs. Updates needing more than 3 frames of work went from 14% to 3.7%, and more than 4 frames from 4.0% to 0.1%. The full record is in docs/optimizations.md. None of this has been on real hardware yet.

2026-11-18 18:19

The game now holds its speed almost everywhere

Late updates across all eight levels are down from 23.8% in the original to 2.0%. Whenever the camera moved 8 pixels, the game re-sent every background tile on screen to video memory, even though 96% of them were already there. It now sends only the new ones. That work also ran with interrupts off for long enough to miss vertical blanks, up to 6% of them on level 5. None are missed now. Nearly all the late updates left are deliberate waits inside animations. The patch still builds from your own ROM, and it's only been tested in emulators.

2026-11-18 17:15

Object graphics are about ten times cheaper

The game was unpacking object tiles bit by bit every time it drew them, which took over half of all CPU time on level 6. The patch now unpacks them ahead of time into a larger, 2 MB ROM, built from your own copy of the game. Late updates fell from 58% to 20% on level 6 and from 41% to 24% on level 5, and the worst stall on those levels went from 11 frames to 7. Across all levels it's 23.8% down to 16.4%. The other levels barely moved, so something else is holding them back. This also turned up a bug in the original game: a slip in its tile search takes the top off the wall torch's flame. The flame is whole again. All of this is tested in emulators only, and not on real hardware.

2026-11-18 16:49

The BlastEm fork is now the reference

The fork is built from source with two bugs fixed: it wouldn't compile without OpenGL, and headless runs counted every frame twice. Both are logged in docs/blastem-fork-fixes.md to offer back later, and nothing has been submitted. The newer upstream DMA fixes weren't needed. The game's DMA only reads work RAM and ROM around 020000 to 03FFFF, never the range those fixes cover. Runs repeat exactly, and turning the profiler on doesn't change video memory on any level. On this build 23.8% of updates ran late, and 58% on level 6. Object graphics cost a little more than PicoDrive counted, so that routine remains the first target.

2026-11-18 16:17

BlastEm agrees with PicoDrive

The same scripted runs now work on BlastEm, a much more exacting emulator. Across all eight levels 23.0% of the game's updates ran late on both, and level by level the figures match within a point, apart from level 2, where the random walks end early at deaths and prompts. The 4-frame gaps PicoDrive showed don't appear on BlastEm, so they look like a PicoDrive quirk. PicoDrive stays in use for profiling, and BlastEm becomes the check on whether an optimization really helps. None of this has been run on a real Genesis yet.

2026-11-18 15:58

The game runs under test, and the first findings

The game now runs headless with scripted input, and a profiling build of PicoDrive shows where its time goes. All eight levels can be reached directly. The game updates once every 5 video frames, 12 times a second. In random walks 23% of those updates came late, and about half on level 6. On levels 5 and 6 the late ones spend much longer building object graphics. On levels 2, 4 and 8 the cause is still unknown. These timings are from the emulator. Three lives per level is confirmed. After the third death the game offers a new game from the very first room, or asks for a certificate, even though it already has a way to reload a level without one. Details are in docs/how-the-game-works.md. The picture shows the eight levels and stays on this machine.

2026-11-18 15:47

The project starts

This began as an idea for a 32X port. The complaints people have about the game are mostly about its design (deaths with little warning, restarting floors, fights that play alike), and the 32X wouldn't fix those, so it's now a straight patch for the Genesis version. The three goals are fairness, bug fixes, and smoother performance where that's possible. The target is The Immortal (USA, Europe), 1 MB, serial T-50186. Its checksum is correct and its header declares no save RAM. Nothing in the game has been examined or changed yet. The game's screenshots and clips will appear here but stay out of git, so they only show on the machine that made them.
